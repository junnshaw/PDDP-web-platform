Connection Tree-Based Variational Autoencoder

ˀ

JT-VAE 介绍

Compared to previous models using molecular graph encoders, the molecular generation model with connective tree variational graph autoencoders is better suited for feature learning and prevents the generation of invalid molecules.

During the encoding phase, the graph encoder and tree encoder encode the molecule separately, yielding two latent representations. This allows us to encode a molecule as a two-part latent representation, where the tree encoder encodes the tree structure and clusters within the tree, and the graph encoder encodes the graph to capture fine-grained connections. The decoding phase then decodes the latent representation back into a molecular graph in two stages. We first use the tree decoder to reconstruct the connectivity tree. Then, we use the graph decoder to predict the fine-grained connections between clusters in the connectivity tree, thereby reconstructing the complete molecular graph. The connectivity tree approach allows us to maintain chemical feasibility during the generation process.

Molecular Tree Decomposition

Using a given molecular graph, we first determine all its simple cycles—edges that do not belong to any cycle. If two simple cycles share two or more overlapping atoms, we merge them, since they form a specific structure known as a bridged compound. Each cycle or edge is considered a cluster. Then, we construct a cluster graph by adding edges between all intersecting clusters. Finally, we can obtain a spanning tree for it.
